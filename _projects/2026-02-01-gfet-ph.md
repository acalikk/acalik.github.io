## Engineering challenge

A pH-dependent transistor response needs a physical explanation before it can support reliable sensing. I investigated how graphene device structure, surface treatment, and measurement conditions influenced that response during my Xsensio internship.

## My contribution

- Characterized GFET pH sensitivity by examining shifts in current–gate-voltage curves and the Dirac voltage.
- Compared devices with and without holes in the graphene and investigated the effect of surface treatment.
- Examined carrier mobility alongside sensitivity to understand the consequences of surface modification.
- Provided a semi-automated framework for analyzing the measurements.

## Approach and decisions

I treated sensitivity and charge transport as linked measurements. A larger response could be useful for sensing while also reflecting changes to the graphene channel. Comparing the electrical characteristics helped me assess those effects together.

I also investigated ionic-strength effects on the measured Dirac voltage. This made the solution conditions part of the characterization, alongside the device itself.

## Results and limits

In the tested devices, holes and surface treatment increased pH sensitivity. The surface-treated devices also showed reduced mobility, highlighting a trade-off between the sensing response and charge transport.

These results describe the conditions and devices studied during the internship. They supported investigation of the response mechanism; a complete predictive model and product validation would require further work.
