## Engineering challenge

Electrochemical measurements can change because of the sensing interface, the sample, or the measurement setup itself. During my internship at Xsensio, I investigated thin-film platinum and gold electrodes for exosome sensing and worked on making repeated experiments more efficient and interpretable.

## My contribution

- Characterized electrode–electrolyte interfaces using cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), examining double-layer capacitance and charge-transfer resistance.
- Automated the experimental setup and data analysis to support repeated measurements and reduce manual work.
- Investigated sources of drift, variation between electrodes, and measurement noise, including the effects of fluid handling.
- Explored lock-in measurements as another way to examine surface-dependent electrical responses.

## Approach and decisions

I compared repeated measurements and stability tests before interpreting changes as a concentration response. When fluid delivery and bubbles interfered with the measurements, I iterated on the automated setup and sample handling. This linked the electrical readout to the physical conditions at the electrode.

The work also required comparing measurement methods. CV, EIS, and lock-in detection provided different views of the interface, which helped identify where further controls and repeatability checks were needed.

## Results and limits

Automation of the setup and analysis improved productivity fourfold. The experiments also identified stability and repeatability as central limitations of the sensing workflow.

The exosome measurements remained exploratory. Reliable detection still required further work on stability, repeatability, and assay performance.
