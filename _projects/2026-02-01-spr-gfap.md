## Engineering challenge

The SenSwiss team developed a surface plasmon resonance (SPR) biosensor concept for glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), a biomarker relevant to traumatic brain injury. The system combined a gold-coated grating, an antibody assay, optical readout, and microfluidic sample delivery.

## My contribution

- Microfabricated sensing surfaces in the cleanroom using gold sputtering and evaporation.
- Investigated PEG-based functionalization strategies to stabilize gold nanoparticles intended for signal amplification.
- Contributed to electronics conception for the prototype.
- Assessed competing technologies and product viability for the business plan.

These were contributions within a multidisciplinary team. The optical design, fluidic cartridge, assay development, and software involved other team members and collaborators.

## Approach and decisions

The team used the existing grating structure of commercial DVD-R discs as the basis for the sensing surface. Depositing thin metal layers on this structure offered a practical route to an optical prototype without fabricating a new grating from scratch.

My fabrication and functionalization work addressed how the sensing surface could be prepared for the assay. Competitor assessment added a second perspective: how the proposed device would fit among existing approaches to biomarker testing.

## Results and limits

The team's August 2023 report measured an optical sensitivity of **972 nm/RIU** using glycerol solutions. This is a refractive-index calibration result, not a GFAP detection limit.

At the report date, optical and biological feasibility had been demonstrated separately. Flow artifacts still complicated measurements, and validation in plasma remained future work. The surface, assay, fluidics, and readout all had to work together before the system could support a reliable biomarker measurement.
